# Body model: her body from the mesh, not from the author's physics rig

Date: 2026-11-25
Status: accepted; implemented for MMD (slices B1 to B5 of the [body model plan](../../../.omo/plans/body-model.md)), and integrated on 2026-11-25 with the arm and hand work that reached `main` first. Every number in Consequences is against that `main`. The resting arm carriage on coat and skirt rigs awaits the owner's visual review.

Current rules are in [Body ownership](../context/body-ownership.md) and the `motion-driver-humanoid` README. This record keeps the evidence and the decisions.

## Context

The arm guard took its obstacles from the rigid bodies the model's author built for cloth physics: the bone-following bodies of the torso, hips, legs and head. Those bodies describe the cloth simulation, not her body.

Two measurements frame this work. Both use 32 MMD characters of the owner's library in the `idle-drive`, `speak` and `walk` scenarios (40 s scored, seed 1, physics on), and count a frame as pierced when an arm's centreline crosses a triangle of her body or clothing that the arm does not wear.

**2026-11-24, before the arm and hand work** (a scratch harness at `5fbad4da0`):

- On 12 rigs an arm rested inside a simulated coat or skirt in 90 % or more of idle frames. The guard never saw simulated cloth, and on 24 of 34 model files the author's collision masks keep fewer than a quarter of the cloth bodies from ever meeting an arm body.
- On 3 rigs a cloth blocker wider than the shoulder span held the shoulder joint. Sparxie and Robin had no clear arm pose on any performer tick; Aha's upper arms rested 81 degrees from vertical.
- The guard's cloth measure (`clothContact`) did not track the surface: 100 % pierced with 0 % contact on Lily, 100 % contact and never pierced on Vivian.

**2026-11-25, on the `main` this work landed on** (`pnpm qa:motion3d clearance` at `c7b1ac5cb`). The arm and hand work had added `BodyCollision.fit`: the corrector shrinks every proxy the rest hang lies in. That freed the trapped rigs before this work landed, and this record does not claim it: no rig has a tick without a clear arm pose, and Aha's arms rest at 18 degrees. The fit does not show the guard her clothing, and it gives way wherever the default hang touches an authored body: on 27 of the 32 rigs it shrinks 1 to 15 bodies (seed 1), to 0.21 of their size at the least. So on that `main` 14 rigs are pierced in 5 % or more of idle frames. Ten are pierced in more than 90 % (Aha, Chisa, two Denia variants, Lily, Aria and her robot form, Ellen, Nicole, Sunna) and four in 29 to 78 % (Robin, Iuno, Evanescia, the third Denia variant).

Five throwaway prototypes showed that no fix which keeps the author's bodies as the description of her body reaches every rig, and that fitting obstacles to the mesh frees the trapped rigs. The plan's section 1 holds their numbers.

The roadmap asks for the same knowledge elsewhere: the VRM adapter must not assume MMD, and props and terrain contact need to know where her surface is.

## Decision

1. **One body model, renderer-neutral.** `buildBodyModel` (`@proj-airi/motion-driver-humanoid/body-model`) builds it once at load from a `BodyMeshSource`: bind-pose vertices, skin weights, triangles, the bone hierarchy, the canonical bones, and the simulated bones. The source names no renderer type. The build is pure and deterministic.
2. **Its parts.** `volumes` are hard obstacles on canonical bones, from the hips to the shoulders and on the thighs: her body, her rigid clothing and flesh on simulated bones. `garments` are simulated geometry with its rest shape. `limbs` are measured arm radii. `rest` is the abduction each resting arm needs and what limited it. `report` names every volume that was narrowed, shrunk or dropped.
3. **No shape of the model comes from the author's physics rig.** The model reads no rigid body's shape, size or rotation. The one place where authored shapes still reach the performer is outside the model: the head and the neck (decision 5). It reads five things from the rig: which bones are simulated, each joint's limits, each joint's position, the centre of each simulated body, and the collision groups and masks. The joints give only the mobility of each simulated bone: a joint lets a body's centre travel by its rotation limit times the centre's distance from the joint, so the centre is the lever the limits act on. The collision groups give only which simulated bones the simulator collides with an arm.

   The lever is an authored value, because many PMX files leave bone tails unset. A lever from the mesh was measured on the ten local rigs (2026-11-25): the centroid of the vertices a bone carries, by skin weight or by dominant bone, with the bone's bind position where it carries none. Eight rigs keep every bone's class and every rest. On both Elysia variants the upper chest bones leave the flesh: their mobility rises from 0.36 of the shoulder span to between 0.50 and 0.62, above the 0.5 that flesh allows, because the skin a chest bone carries lies on the surface, farther from the joint than the mass its author placed. 160 to 313 flesh vertices then become a garment; with the centroid by skin weight, one variant loses the chest bone of her right side only. The mesh lever is not adopted.
4. **Flesh is a short simulated chain.** A simulated chain at most two bones deep that moves at most half the shoulder span is flesh and joins the hard volumes. A deeper chain is a garment. Joint limits alone do not separate the two: several rigs lock every cloth joint and still let the cloth trail on soft joints, and chest bones measure 0.10 to 0.35 of the span.

   **A garment is one piece.** The first build took every chain under one unsimulated bone for one garment, so a skirt and a bow that hang from the same hips were one: the bow gained the arm collisions of decision 10 when only the skirt's author let an arm body push it, and the rest solve and the pinch rule read one hull around both. Chains under the hips or a spine bone are now one garment only when a triangle of the mesh has corners on both. Chains under any other bone stay one garment, because the author gave them a bone of their own. Measured on the ten local rigs (2026-11-25), the mesh keeps every skirt whole (13 to 28 chains each), and the rigs' 27 garments become 55: Ellen's four belts and her tail part from her skirt, Carlotta's cape from her dress, Elysia's two bells and her crystal from each other. Elysia's bells were pinched only as one hull across her hips; apart, neither holds an arm. The skeleton rule exists because of the bound of decision 7: tied by the mesh alone, one of Iuno's dress panels parts from the belt behind it, its own rest shape clears one degree later (16 degrees, not 15), the 3 degrees of sway then need 19 degrees, one more than the 6 degree range allows, and the arm settles at 16, where it is pierced in 41 % of idle frames (none at 18).
5. **The fit.** The trunk is fitted in height slices, one capsule a slice, lying along the longer of the slice's width and depth. Each thigh gets a capsule along the bone. Beside the upper arm, down to an upper arm's length below the shoulders, a hard volume ends inside the shoulder joints.

   **The model ends at the shoulders.** The head and the neck keep the author's kinematic bodies, built and fitted as on `main` (`buildMMDHeadColliders`, `BodyCollision.fit`): the performer's colliders are the model's volumes followed by those bodies, and the arm gate's head list is the same bodies. Two fits of the head were built and measured before this was decided. Slices up to the crown removed `headClearance` on 8 rigs, because the corrector sizes its head probe by how far the head's colliders reach from the head bone. One sphere on the middle of the head's extents kept the probe short. Against `main`, on the 32 rigs over six seeds, it still lost the hair tuck on 8 rigs and gained it on 3, lost `headClearance` on 5 and gained it on 4, moved the thinking hand's keys on the QA rig (the held upper arm 54 to 83 degrees abducted, from 37 to 43), which failed the lab criterion `emotion.minSeparationFromNeutral` (1.886 under a limit of 2, from 2.011), and gave the chin no surface on two of the ten local rigs. The hands go to the head for the thinking hand, the hair tuck and the covered mouth; `main` tuned those against the author's head and neck bodies, and they need a skull, a jaw and a face, which one sphere is not. With the author's bodies every rig keeps the `hairTouch` and the `headClearance` it has on `main`.

   A neck capsule along the bone was fitted with the sphere and removed with it. The rest solve gives the same rests, limits, pinches and adjustments on all 32 rigs without the head and neck volumes; beside the author's bodies the capsule changes no capability on any rig and seed; and every one of the 32 rigs carries 1 to 5 authored neck bodies, so it would cover the neck twice.
6. **The resting arm always clears the hard volumes.** The rest solve takes the smallest abduction from the default 12 degrees up to 22 at which the performer's own arm test clears them. A volume that no such abduction clears shrinks until one does. A wide shell can therefore never hold an arm, whatever the mesh.
7. **Garments open the rest within bounds.** Garments add up to 6 degrees, and a free-swinging garment 3 degrees of sway more. An arm whose centreline lies inside a free-swinging garment opens up to 28 degrees, to the smallest abduction that takes it out of every such garment, or else leaves it inside the fewest. When no abduction inside the 6 degrees clears the garments, the arm settles up to 4 degrees out, where its capsule overlaps them least. A garment that still holds an arm's centreline at its final rest, after decision 8, is pinched. Each arm's `rest.limit` names what still holds it: a hard volume that had to shrink (decision 6) comes first, and otherwise the garment the arm's capsule still overlaps, pinched or not. Aria's first form has one pinched garment and reports a volume on both arms. The garments are tested together, as in decision 8. The first build opened the arm for one garment at a time, so the arm could leave one and end inside another it had been clear of, which the model then did not report as pinched. No library rig has that case: both builds give the same model on all 32 (2026-11-25).
8. **A garment the simulator collides with the arm is cleared as a body is,** up to 30 degrees, the widest rest the removed runtime solver allowed. The simulator pushes such a garment off the arm on every step, so an arm that leans on its edge keeps it moving. Such garments are cleared together, at the first abduction that clears them all: cleared one after another, the arm could open past one into another, and the rest then depended on their order.
9. **The MMD adapter reads the mesh.** `readMMDBodyModel` builds the source from the mesh geometry, the rig profile and the PMX joints. `MMDBody` hands the model to the performer as two rig traits: the volumes as the first entries of `BodyRigTraits.colliders`, before the author's head and neck bodies (decision 5), and each arm's rest abduction as `BodyArmTraits.restAbduct` (decision 12). The solver that lifted the arms while frames ran is removed with its warm-up and its persistence. The debug hang override still works.
10. **The simulator gets the arms.** `prepareMMDSecondaryMotion` runs before `mmd.setPhysics` in the scene and in the headless loader. It lets every arm body collide with the garments whose author already let some arm body push them, and changes no other pair. Three cases gain nothing and stay as authored. A pinched garment keeps the pairs its author gave it. A simulated body with an empty mask is a weight or an anchor, not the garment's surface. A rig with fewer free collision groups than its arms use is not changed at all (`no-free-group`), and when the free groups run out partway, the garment bodies left over are counted as skipped in a report that still reads `applied`.
11. **`clothContact` still reads the author's simulated bodies** and stays information only.
12. **The rest hang is a trait of the rig.** The corrector on `main` fits each proxy to the rest hang, and every arm form starts from the rest pose. While the model's rest reached the performer as a per-tick lift on top of the default 12 degrees, both read the default hang. Fitted there, the corrector shrank 1 to 6 of the model's volumes on 30 of the 32 rigs, which undoes decision 6 exactly where the model had opened the rest. So each arm rests at `BodyArmTraits.restAbduct`: the performer's seeded rest pose, `restChannels()`, the reference `BodyCollision` fits its proxies to, and the pose the arm forms start from all carry it. The rest solve measures the volumes unfitted (`new BodyCollision(traits, rest, false)`), because the fit would shrink what the solve opens the arm for. `MMDBody` scales the arm gate's copies of the volumes and of the head and neck bodies by the corrector's fit, entry by entry in the order of the colliders, as `main` does for authored bodies, and the collider overlay draws those copies. `armLift` stays a per-tick input for the debug hang override only, as an offset from the rig's rest.

    The fit then finds little, and not nothing. It shrinks no head or neck body on any rig, here or on `main`. Over six seeds a rig it shrinks no volume on 22 rigs. On the other 10 it shrinks 1 to 3 volumes on some seeds (43 of 192 rig and seed pairs), to 0.86 of their size at the least and 0.96 at the median. Each is a thigh, hip or waist volume that itself limits the rest, on a rig no garment opened further. Two causes share the 60 cases evenly: the seed draws the hang up to 2 degrees inside the solved one, and the fit keeps a settled key's headroom of 0.05 of the segment where the rest solve keeps 0.03. In 7 cases the seed's rest pose is inside the arm test's own margin, by 0.03 model units at most.

## Consequences

Measured on 2026-11-25 with `pnpm qa:motion3d clearance` over the 32 rigs and three scenarios (40 s scored, seed 1), this work against the `main` it landed on (`c7b1ac5cb`). The limits are those of [Testing and QA](../context/testing.md).

- **Idle.** Of the 14 rigs pierced at idle on `main`, 11 are never pierced: Aha, Evanescia, Robin, Chisa, the three Denia variants, Iuno, Lily, Ellen and Sunna. 29 rigs are never pierced at idle, from 16. Aria and her robot form stay at 100 %, and Nicole goes from 96 % to 73 %; Known limits explains the three. No rig is newly pierced.
- **No rig has a tick without a clear arm pose,** as on `main`.
- **Speak and walk.** No rig is pierced more than 1.8 points above `main` (Alice, `walk`). The 18 rigs under 5 % in every scenario on `main` stay under 2 %. Chisa's `speak` goes from 80 % to 36 %, Sunna's `walk` from 56 % to 3 %, and the robot form's `walk` from 98 % to 50 %.
- **Arm carriage.** Two rigs break the limit of 8 degrees at idle: Aha's mean upper-arm angle rises by 11.3 degrees (17.9 to 29.2) and Evanescia's by 9.8 (16.0 to 25.8). Decision 8 opens both for a garment the simulator collides with the arm, and both were pierced on `main` (100 % and 51 %). The limit is set at idle; in the other scenarios Aha is also 8.7 degrees further out in `speak` and 10.4 in `walk`, and Evanescia 8.7 in `walk`. Every other rig rises by 6.9 degrees at most at idle (Ellen, Vivian, Sunna). Eight rigs rest further in, one Elysia variant by 5.7 degrees, where the runtime solver on `main` had lifted the arms.
- **Cloth.** At idle no rig's share of reversing cloth vertices rises by more than 1.1 points (Lily), and Ellen's falls from 3.2 % to 0.0 %. One row breaks the limit of 2 points: Sunna's `walk` rises by 2.4 (4.7 % to 7.1 %). Her arms walk 7 degrees further out and swing back fully, beside the bag her author lets the arms push, where on `main` they passed through her skirt in 56 % of walking frames.
- **The walking arm's back swing.** The corrector probes each arm's room behind the hip from the rest pose. On `main` (seed 1) the default hang left no room on one arm or both on 25 rigs, so that arm's back swing was eased into 4 degrees. From the model's rest both arms have the full room on 24 rigs. Both Yuzuha rigs lose the room one arm had (17 and 16 degrees), and six more rigs stay without room on one arm or both, because their rest clears a thigh by less than the gait's skin.
- **The guard's obstacle list** shrinks from 16 to 88 authored bodies a rig to 12 to 31: 9 to 11 fitted volumes and the author's 3 to 22 kinematic head and neck bodies, beside 1 to 12 garments. 18 rigs get the arm collisions of decision 10, 12 have no garment that an arm body pushes, and 2 have no free collision group. One of the 18, a Remielle variant, runs out of groups and leaves 6 garment bodies as authored.
- **Capabilities,** over six seeds a rig. `gestures`, `headClearance` and the hair tuck (`hairTouch`) are the same as on `main` on every rig and seed, because the head and the neck are the author's bodies on both sides: 27 rigs reach the hair tuck, and the five that do not (both Lucilla forms, Sigrika and two Remielle variants) do not on `main` either. One capability differs: `clasp` is lost on Chisa in her swimsuit. Her arms reach it from the default hang, where the corrector shrinks four of her volumes, and not from the model's rest.
- **Emotion forms.** On the QA rig (Chisa; `emotions`, seeds 1 and 2) the mean hold magnitude of the four arm-form emotions stays within 4 % of `main`: flustered 232 to 225 degrees, love 223 to 217, sad 213 to 206, think 217 to 218. The arms start 7 degrees further out. On the ten local rigs (`emotions`, seed 1) the lowest ratio to `main` is 0.88 (Ellen, sad) and the highest 1.11 (Iuno, sad).
- **Lab gate on the QA rig** (12 scenarios at seed 1 and `emotions` seeds 2 to 6): no gated criterion's verdict differs from `main`; 212 pass and 23 fail on both sides. `emotion.minSeparationFromNeutral`, pooled over the six emotion runs, is 2.100 over a limit of 2, from 2.011: the pooled sigma is 9.54 degrees, from 9.98, and the three `think` trials spread by 14.3 degrees rms, from 15.0. The thinking hand's fingertip key is searched against the head's proxies, which are the author's bodies on both sides. `walk-mood-arc` `bilateral.upperArm.peakCorr`, which flipped against the earlier `main`, fails on both sides now (0.983 and 0.983 against a limit of 0.914).
- **Cost.** On the QA rig, best of three runs a side: the pose stages' summed 95th percentile is 1.00 ms on `main` and 1.00 ms here in `idle-drive`, and 1.38 and 1.39 ms in `walk`. The physics step's 95th percentile is 19.1 and 19.5 ms in `idle-drive`, and 22.0 and 22.5 ms in `walk`. Other work loaded the machine during these runs, and the QA rig is not the heaviest rig of the library; Known limits says what this leaves open. Building the model at load takes 15 to 85 ms on the ten local rigs (20,000 to 130,000 vertices), and reading the mesh 3 to 26 ms.

## Known limits

- **Aria and her robot form stay pierced at idle (100 %, as on `main`).** Their dress is a pinched garment: the resting arm cannot leave it within 28 degrees.
- **Nicole stays pierced in 73 % of idle frames (96 % on `main`).** A plush doll and a bow hang at her right hip. The arm's capsule leaves their bind shape at 22 degrees and needs 25 with the sway of decision 7. Her arm clears the hard volumes at 15, so the 6 degree range ends at 21, and the arm settles at 19, leaning on them. While every chain under the hips was one garment (before decision 4's pieces), the wider hull opened her right arm to 25 degrees; that state was never swept with the official command.
- **A garment whose sway clearance lies just outside the 6 degree range gets none of it.** Decision 7 then settles the arm where it leaves the bind shape, up to 4 degrees out. Nicole is the measured case. Iuno's dress panel would rest at 16 degrees for a need of 19 if it were a garment of its own; it rests at 18 because it shares a bone and a hull with the belt behind it.
- **Eleven rigs report a rest limit, where the plan allowed three.** The three above stay pierced. The other eight are never pierced at idle. On Robin, Denia in her white dress, Lily, Sunna and Vivian the resting arm's capsule still overlaps a garment's rest shape, without its centreline inside. On Alice and both Yuzuha rigs the rest solve shrank a volume (the ornament limit below). Read as written, the plan's bound of three limited rigs is not met; three rigs stay pierced.
- **Aha and Evanescia rest 11 and 10 degrees further out than on `main`,** over the sweep's limit of 8. Decision 8 clears a skirt and a waist bow that the simulator collides with their arms.
- **Sunna's cloth reverses 2.4 points more in `walk`,** over the sweep's limit of 2.
- **Gesture paths do not avoid garments.** Chisa is pierced in 36 % of `speak` frames, from 80 %: her forearm crosses the coat during gestures, and her author keeps coat and arms apart in the simulation.
- **The head and the neck are not in the model.** There the arms clear the author's kinematic bodies, so what a hand finds at the hair, the cheek and the chin is what the author built, as on `main`, and a rig whose author built a wide hair-collision body or none at the jaw keeps that. Decision 5 gives what one sphere on the head cost. A head fitted to the mesh needs a skull, a jaw and a face, and gestures tuned against it; it is a later reader of the model.
- **The corrector's fit still shrinks a model volume on 10 rigs,** by 14 % at most (decision 12). Closing that needs the rest solve to clear the seed's range and a settled key's headroom, which would open those rests by about 2 degrees.
- **A skirt and a bow under a bone of their own are still one garment,** whatever the mesh says.
- **One capsule a slice cannot hold a body and an ornament.** On Alice, both Yuzuha rigs and both Aria forms the rest solve shrinks a volume for that reason, and bind-shape silhouettes are off by 2 to 4 degrees on Iuno, Carlotta and Sigrika.
- **The measure counts centreline crossings.** Sleeves are about twice as thick as the corrector's arm capsule on the median rig, so shallow overlap of sleeve and clothing is not counted and not addressed.
- **Clips are not steered.** On the earlier `main`, thirty generic VMD clips pierced 23 to 86 % of frames on the rigs that were pierced, and this work does not change how a clip moves the arms.
- **No desktop performance bench compares this work with `main`.** `pnpm qa:bench` ran 15 launches at this work's head and rejected every one as `host-contended`, because other work loaded the machine, and it did not start at `main`. The headless stage timings under Consequences stand in for it and do not measure the desktop app.
- **The cost is measured on the QA rig only.** The plan asked for the heaviest rig. The QA rig also gets no new arm collisions, because decision 10 finds no garment on it that an arm body pushes, so its physics timing does not show what those collisions cost on the 18 rigs that get them. No controlled run compares such a rig with `main`.
- The head, the neck, hair, sleeves, what hangs from a leg, the shins and the feet are not fitted.

## Not decided

- Whether the garment bounds become a style parameter of the arm carriage.
- When the desktop performance bench and a heaviest-rig timing run are repeated on a calm machine.
- Whether the rest solve clears the seed's range and a settled key's headroom, so that the corrector's fit finds nothing among the model's volumes.
- The later readers of the model: a head fitted to the mesh (decision 5), which then replaces the author's head and neck bodies; the VRM adapter (milestone M5), which has no authored bodies to fall back on for the head; props and terrain contact (milestone M4); touch; the corrector's arm radius from `limbs`; and the secondary motion boundary for another physics backend.

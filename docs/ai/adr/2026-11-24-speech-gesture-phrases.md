# Speech gestures play in phrases

Date: 2026-11-24
Status: accepted. Changes the unit structure of architecture 4a and plan 3.1's decision D3 (one gesture unit per utterance) in the [whole-body performer](2026-11-11-whole-body-performer.md)'s gesture layer.

## Context

A visual audit of speech found that she gestures in one repeated pose: the elbow parks at the waist, the wrist delivers the beats, and the feet, ribs and free shoulder take little part. The fingers keep one spread through every stroke. Every stroke of a unit left from and returned to one ready key, a unit covered a whole utterance, and the only body participation was each stroke's torso pulse and head nod. Energy alone scaled the gestures; mood did not reach them.

## Decision

1. **A unit plays phrases.** A phrase has its hands, a place (`centre`, `low`, `side`, `high`, or two hands' `inner`), a theme and two to four strokes, drawn from the phrase stream (`deriveSeed(seed, 142)`). The place and the theme differ from the phrase before. At a phrase's end the hands go on into the next phrase, prepared again from where they are to its place, the other hand taking over or both joining; a hand that leaves retracts. The unit's marker (`unitSeq`) counts units, not phrases.
2. **Hands lower between thoughts.** 0.4 s of quiet lowers the hands (4a had 0.8 s), so a pause between phrases ends the unit. From drive 0.6 up the speaker holds the hands through up to 0.4 s + 0.6 s x drive of quiet instead, and hands at rest in a phrase that has stroked go on to the next phrase's place in the pause; with every pause lowering the hands, an excited speaker lifted and dropped them between phrases without a stroke. A unit lowered while speech goes on (a pause, a cap, a phrase's end) takes a refractory of 0.4 s + 1.2 s x (1 - drive); after speaking ends the refractory stays 2.5 s + 3 s x (1 - drive). Below drive 0.4 a phrase's end may lower the hands too.
3. **Drive replaces energy.** Gesture size, tempo, form shares and phrase choices read the drive, the energy raised 0.3 x arousal and 0.1 x valence. Below drive 0.4 a unit opens only on an emphasis or a loud onset, and an ordinary onset does not stroke, so a calm speaker gestures seldom.
4. **The elbow floats and the hand lives.** Each recovery returns to a ready key moved within the place, the elbow up or down by turns. A theme stroke sets the phrase's hand: its beats keep that palm. A beat's hand closes part-way to a cup, every hold eases the hand back part-way, and the recovery opens it. Because beats now keep other palms and leave from several places, the chop cuts nearly straight down to stay a form of its own. A hand carried on into a new phrase, or joining one, always takes the corrected transition and homes in on its new place; the hand-offs added plain moves, which alone read smoother than the talk reference.
5. **The body takes part.** An additive third gesture layer holds the phrase's trunk turn toward a one-handed phrase's hand, a forward lean with drive, a head and neck tilt, the gesturing shoulder's lift and the free shoulder's settle. Each stroke lifts its shoulder, or presses it down on a downward stroke. Standing, a unit lowering its hands asks for the weight on the other foot through `plan.support`, unless an earlier layer holds it.

## Consequences

- "Every onset strokes" no longer holds inside an utterance: onsets in a pause, before a calm unit opens and in a lowered unit's refractory pass without a stroke.
- Stroke keys clear the settling headroom where they can, because the trunk now moves under them. A segment's minimum duration now counts the velocity it carries, which a corrected transition's second submovement can bring past the followers' input slope.
- `createGestureLayers` returns three layers; hosts register all three.
- `lab:motion3d report gesture-timing` still judges the `speak` scenario, whose utterances carry no pauses. The `speak-phrases` scenario carries pauses for visual review; no report judges it.

## Amendment, 2026-11-25

A visual audit of this decision found hands that rose and dropped with nothing stressed, and hands that waited at ready height after their last stroke.

- **A hand rises only to say something.** Past the speaking edge a unit opens only on an onset that strokes. An onset that opens a unit, or that finds the lead hand still preparing, strokes as soon as the preparation lets it, within 0.6 s of the onset, and the unit lowers its hands only after that stroke. Before, the onset that opened a unit passed while the hands rose, and a pause or the end of speech lowered them with no stroke at all. Such a stroke lands up to one preparation after its onset, outside the 200 ms window that `gesture-timing` counts as a hit. A phrase that ends on the unit's sixth stroke lowers the hands instead of going on into another phrase, and in a two-hand phrase a hand that has risen and not stroked yet joins the lead's beat as a two-hand beat. Before, the other hand could rise into a phrase that the stroke cap left no stroke for, and the second hand of a phrase of beats rose and waited without a stroke, because a beat takes one hand.
- **A raised hand lets go when it has nothing more to stress.** A hand that waits at its ready key for 1.1 s of speech with no stroke leaving retracts while speech goes on. When the other hand still strokes, only the waiting hand retracts and the unit continues one-handed. Quiet in a pause does not count toward the wait, because the pause rule already decides whether hands hold through a pause. A hand that takes over a phrase from the other hand starts its wait again. Before, a hand that had taken a thought's last stroke, or a hand left out of a one-hand phrase, waited with the elbow bent until speaking ended or the unit hit a cap. The wait is longer than the speak scenario's widest peak gap (1.5 s) less a beat, its shortest hold and its recovery (0.46 s at drive 0.85), so hands stay up between close strokes and do not rise and drop on every beat. A first wait of 1 s + 0.4 s x drive kept a hand still at ready for up to 1.45 s after a thought's last stroke; a wait of 1 s let a lively hand go just before a stroke 1.5 s after the one before.
- **Consecutive beats differ.** A hand's beats take three stroke paths in turn: the form's own path (down and forward), a shallower path further forward, and a diagonal path out to the arm's side. All three are about the same length, so the amplitude calibration holds. Each recovery's float now moves the elbow at least half its range, up or down by turns. The hand goes back as the elbow rises and forward as it falls, so a lowered hand stays in front of the body and does not return to the same spot in front of the hip. Before, one consecutive beat pair in ten repeated the last beat's direction within 1.5 degrees.
- **The ready hand is half open.** Outside a set phrase, a hand at its ready key is half way from the rest hand to the open hand, which is the same hand a set phrase holds. The rest hand curls most at the middle joints. Held palm down in front of the body it reads as a claw, and a beat's hold softened back toward it.
- **The palm-up offer reaches toward the listener.** The palm-up stroke changes from `[0.1, -0.02, 0.08]` to `[0.07, -0.01, 0.1]` A. From a phrase's side place, the old offer went more out than forward (0.7 to 0.9 times as far forward as out) and ended lower and closer than the offer before the phrases. It now goes 1.4 to 1.7 times as far forward as out, and its hit moves 0.045 arm lengths forward, 0.07 in and 0.02 up. The vector is 5.5 % shorter than the old one, because a longer forward reach cannot land inside the gesture-timing window at the softest followers (tension 0, energy 0.85).
- **The other hand takes a phrase whose ready key does not clear.** A one-hand unit whose hand cannot reach its ready key from where it hangs, because the path from the hang meets the body, opens with the other free hand. Before, the unit did not open at all: in `speak-phrases` on `chisa-swimsuit` the left hand's centre key did not clear from its narrow hang, and nothing moved from 32.1 to 35.7 s while she spoke.
- **A weak onset leans the hands.** Past the speaking edge an onset too weak to stroke opens a unit whose hands move 0.3 of the way to their ready keys over their preparation time: the elbows bend and the hands come a little forward, which reads as a shift of the hang, not a raised hand. The next stroking onset raises them the rest of the way, in the rest of that time, and they stroke it. Leaning hands with no stroking onset lower after 1.1 s of speech, as ready hands do, and such a unit leaves no refractory, because the hands did not gesture. Before, a weak onset opened nothing: speech that resumed on one (`speak-phrases` at 32.06 s, voice level 0.29 against the stroke rule's 0.3) left the hands still until the first stroking onset, whose stroke then came up to a whole preparation late.
- **The hair tuck reaches through a via pose.** The idle hair touch uses the speech layer's key solver, so this amendment records it here. From rest the arm passes a via pose: the elbow is 0.62 of the way to the touch key, the flex 0.75, and the abduction and twist 0.2. The hand comes up in front of the chest and then out to the temple, and the way home goes back through the same pose. The reach keeps its duration, because the act starts the sweep at 0.8 s. A stronger elbow lead would break the act layer's easing limit of 0.25 degrees per tick per tick. Straight from rest the wrist swung 0.3 to 0.4 arm lengths out beyond the touch, like a wave; it now swings 0.15 to 0.24.
